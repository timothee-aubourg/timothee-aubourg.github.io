I work on extracting clinical signal from digital measurements in Parkinson's disease: smartphone-based tests, wearable accelerometry, neuromotor assessments, questionnaires, and clinical records. I also work on the validation needed before those measures can be used clinically.

My doctoral work (Orange Labs / Université Grenoble-Alpes, 2017–2020) looked at passive mobile phone data as a source of behavioural biomarkers, showing associations between circadian rhythm and social asymmetry measures and depression in older adults ([publications](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=y9gR9AYAAAAJ); [patent](https://hal.science/hal-04825788/)). A subsequent postdoc extended this to wearable-based gait phenotyping. My work at Oxford has two main strands.

## Motor trajectory stratification and disease progression

Parkinson's disease progresses very differently between patients. I use unsupervised machine learning to stratify motor trajectories, grouping patients by the shape and speed of their progression. I then test whether smartphone-based clinical assessments taken at a single baseline visit can predict which group a patient falls into. This is the subject of [a 2026 paper in *BMJ Neurology Open*](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjno-2026-001740).

## Composite scores and clinical validation of digital tools

The [Oxford Parkinson's Disease Centre](https://www.dpag.ox.ac.uk/opdc) has a long track record in composite scores for Parkinson's disease, which fuse measurements across domains under clinical and domain constraints, an approach [established in the literature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35387867/). I extend this to smartphone-based clinical assessments, with the aim of scores that hold up against current clinical standards while remaining usable outside a research clinic. This work draws on a 20+ site collaboration assembling multimodal clinical and digital data. [Our work on smartphone-based dopaminergic prediction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-02148-2) reports early results on a subcohort.

## Tooling

I build agent-based tooling for my own analysis work: data quality checks, pipeline automation, and exploratory analysis across heterogeneous multi-site datasets. I was a finalist in the agentic AI track of the [Oxford Clinical AI Hackathon](https://enspire.ox.ac.uk/event/oxford-clinical-ai-hackathon-6-8th-april-2026) (2026).

## Collaborations

The limiting resource in this field is data: diverse, longitudinal, well-characterised, from real patient populations. Everyday devices also make assessment possible where access to specialist neurological care is limited, which is part of why the collaboration spans the sites it does.

My work at Oxford coordinates research across Oxford, Cambridge, University College London, University of Washington / Mayo Clinic / [NAPS cohort](https://www.naps-rbd.org/) (US), Montpellier Clinique Beau Soleil (France), Chinese University of Hong Kong, Seoul National University Hospital (South Korea), University of Melbourne / [APM](https://cureparkinsons.org.uk/2019/01/the-apm/) (Australia), and clinical sites of the [TrapCaf project](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37858118/) across Egypt, Ethiopia, Ghana, Kenya, Nigeria, and Tanzania.
